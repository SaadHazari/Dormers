Onboarding is the most important stretch of a customer's life with us: the journey from "who's Dormers?" to a warm meal landing at their door — and them wanting the next one. Why onboarding is worth understanding properly

It has three doors.
A normal sign-up, a staff/intern claim, and a referral gift. They share one engine but start very differently — and the referral door skips the wizard entirely.
Real money shows up early.
Actual cards, coupons, and credits. A handful of guards make sure nobody's double-charged and no price can be fiddled with.
It's half human, half clockwork.
Some steps happen the instant you tap; others wait for a scheduled "tick" — like activating a plan at midnight or counting a delivery at 8 PM, all on Dubai time.
By design The customer's app (the dashboard) is intentionally light and calm; the marketing site is intentionally dark and bold — an interface and a brochure are two different jobs. The sign-up wizard is locked light; the referral page is locked dark. That's a choice, not a bug to "fix".

Everyone ends up in the same place — a verified account with a plan — but they walk in through one of three entrances. Knowing which door someone used tells you most of what you need to know about their experience.

Which door did this person come through?

Staff

The sign-up wizard, step by step

The wizard asks one thing at a time and feels more like a friendly chat than a form — it's the only place in the product where we lean into emoji. Most choices move you along on their own; a couple have a Continue button. And because we save your progress as you go, a refresh never costs you anything. There are 7 steps — or 8 for "Religious Preference" folks, who get one extra screen.

Did they pick "Religious Preference"?
Yes
An extra screen appears: pick which days are vegetarian (the rest are non-veg). That's the 8th step.
No
Skip straight past it — 7 steps total, no veg-days screen.
By design · why the order looks odd We ask "5 or 6 days?" before the veg-days screen on purpose. The veg-days picker only lets you choose up to one less than your week length — so settling the week first avoids an annoying "oops, too many days" correction later.
1
What do you eat? 🍗 🥗 ☪️
Non-Vegetarian, Veg, or "Religious Preference" (a mix of veg and non-veg days). This one pick shapes their price and what the kitchen makes for them.
Saved as: meal_preference_type · moves on automatically
2
How many days a week? 📅
Mon–Sat (6 days) is the standard rhythm — one day off (Sunday). Mon–Fri (5 days) is lighter and cheaper.
Saved as: week_type · moves on automatically

Pick the vegetarian days. The count caps at one less than the week (so a 6-day customer picks 1–5 veg days). This is the bonus 8th screen.

Any of Nuts, Dairy, Gluten, Shellfish, Eggs, Soy — or "None". Pick as many as apply.

Proving it's really you

We check identity twice — the WhatsApp number first, then the email. Both use a 6-digit code that works only once and expires fast. It's not red tape: it stops fake sign-ups and protects our delivery promise, because we only ever message a number we know is real.

The WhatsApp code, start to finish
What if WhatsApp can't deliver the code?
It worked
Phone is verified. Straight on to the email step.
It failed
We offer to continue by email. The account is created, but the phone is flagged unverified until they confirm it before their first meal.
Used once, then dead.
A code can't be reused to sign up someone else once it's been spent.
Short-lived.
Codes expire quickly, with a cooldown and an hourly limit to deter abuse.
Never delivered blind.
We never schedule a meal to an unverified number — the fallback just defers the phone check, it doesn't skip it.

The account that gets created

The moment that email code checks out, two things happen at once: they get a login, and we save a customer profile with everything the wizard collected. We also mint a short, dorm-based customer ID (the "CID") that follows them everywhere — on labels, orders, and referrals.

What we keep about a new customer

What we keepWhy it matters
name, email, whatsapp_numberWho they are and how we reach them. The phone carries a "verified" flag and timestamp.
cidTheir short ID, built from their dorm — used on meal labels, orders, and referral links.
meal_preference_typeNon-Veg / Veg / Religious — drives both price and what the kitchen cooks.
week_type, veg_days5 or 6 days a week; and, for a mix, which days are vegetarian.
allergens, spice_level_preferenceKitchen safety and taste.
dorm_name, out_of_zoneWhere to deliver. An "Other" dorm sets an out-of-zone flag that pauses checkout until we confirm we cover it.
By design · out of zone Picking an unlisted dorm marks the account out of zone. They can finish signing up, but checkout waits until ops confirms we deliver there — a deliberate guardrail, not a glitch.
Safeguard · already a member? Try to sign up with an email that already exists and you're quietly sent to the login page — we never reveal whether an email is registered.
Rule · staff get linked automatically If this person came through the staff door, we link their new account to their staff record the instant it's created — but only if the email matches, the verified phone matches, and they finished inside the 60-minute claim window. When it matches, they're sent to the staff plan page instead of the normal dashboard.

The "Religious Preference" mix, in one line

For a mixed week, the per-meal price is a blend of veg and non-veg days — more veg days, lower price. As a feel for it, Monthly Premium runs from about AED 22/meal (mostly non-veg) down to AED 17/meal (mostly veg), set by how many veg days they choose.

By design · the hidden staff plan Interns get a private Staff Monthly plan: the 5-day week is free (it's their pay), and the 6-day week is a flat AED 80 — the 4 Saturdays prepaid at AED 20 each. It never shows on the public grid and only works for active staff. (Full story in §10.)
Rule · prices can't drift The price the customer sees, the amount sent to the card, and the server's "is this amount legit?" check all read from the same source. Change a price in the admin panel and all three move together — there's no second price list to fall out of sync.

Checkout & payment

They pick a start date and pay. Out of sight, we roll any credits and loyalty discounts into a single coupon, double-check the price against the official rate, and — if credit covers the whole thing — skip the card payment entirely.

Does their credit cover the whole order?
Covers 100%
Free checkout. No card needed — we create the subscription straightaway. They still get every confirmation.
Some left to pay
Card payment. Off to the secure card page; the coupon brings the total down to what's owed.
From “Pay” to a live subscription
Rule · they only ever see the net The customer sees the price after credit. Behind the scenes we keep the full price and apply credit and loyalty as a coupon — credit is never quietly pre-subtracted on the way in.
Heads-up · the 2 PM kitchen cut-off Start dates respect a 2 PM Dubai kitchen cut-off (no same-day starts after it for new customers) and a 30-day window; non-delivery days are skipped automatically.
Safeguard · checkout will stop you for a reason Before any charge, checkout blocks if the profile's incomplete, the dorm is out of zone, a plan is paused, or a future plan is already queued (only one waiting at a time). Each one prevents a charge we couldn't cleanly honour.

Right after they pay

The second a payment clears, we want the customer to feel looked after. Three messages go out almost at once — each on its own track, so if one stumbles it never holds up the others, and none is ever sent twice.

1 WhatsApp
"You're all set"
A WhatsApp with their plan, total, and first delivery date — within seconds.
2 Email
A branded confirmation
A proper Dormers email with the order number, meals, and start date.
3 Invoice
A tax invoice
A compliant invoice, created and emailed — held back about 2 minutes so it doesn't pile on top of the other two.
Rule · exactly once, no matter what We drop a checkpoint after the important work, and each message has its own "sent" mark. If the card processor re-sends the same event (it happens), nothing doubles up — no second subscription, no repeat texts.
By design · the invoice waits its turn The tax invoice is scheduled a couple of minutes out and picked up by a regular job, rather than fired off instantly. It spaces out the customer's inbox and keeps the payment moment snappy.

Meanwhile, the customer is back on the dashboard looking at a cheerful "Checkout successful" summary. The WhatsApp and email arrive within seconds; the invoice PDF follows shortly after.

Going live: from "scheduled" to first meal

A new plan often starts as Scheduled — set to begin on a future date. It becomes a real, cooking-and-delivering subscription through a few small jobs that run on a clock every day, all on Dubai time.

▸
Start day, just after midnight — it wakes up
A nightly job flips the plan from Scheduled to Active the moment its start date arrives. (The same job also handles skips, pauses, and finished cycles.)
status tick · 00:05 Dubai time
✉
Start day, 9 AM — "Today's the day"
A warm welcome email goes to everyone whose plan starts today — sent once.
start-day emails · 09:00 Dubai time
🍱
Every delivery day, 8 PM — the meal counts
An evening job marks one meal delivered for each active subscriber on a delivery day, and logs the cost for the books.
delivery tick · 20:00 Dubai time
👨‍🍳
The kitchen sees them coming
The kitchen view tallies active subscribers by veg vs non-veg (respecting the mix, plus any skips or pauses) so exactly the right number of each meal gets cooked.
An estimate before the 2 PM cut-off, confirmed after
By design · Dubai time, all year Dubai never changes its clocks, so the schedule is rock-steady. (For the technically minded: midnight Dubai is 8 PM UTC.)
Heads-up · the welcome email won't back-fill The "Today's the day" email goes out for plans starting today only. If a scheduled job ever skips a day, it won't go back and send late.

The staff & intern path

Interns are paid partly in meals. They onboard like everyone else, but with a claim code at the front and a private staff plan at the end — plus admin-approved renewals and a fair refund if they leave early.

1
Invite
An admin adds the intern in the admin panel, which mints a one-time claim code (good for 7 days) and sends it over WhatsApp.
2
Claim
The intern enters their email + code, which opens a 60-minute window to finish onboarding.
3
Onboard
They go through the normal wizard. As the account is created, their staff record links up automatically — email and verified phone have to match.
4
Pick the staff plan
Two choices, below.
Which staff week do they want?
5-day
Free. Set up instantly, no payment — this is their pay.
6-day
AED 80 for the 4 prepaid Saturdays, paid by card like anyone else.
By design · prepay, don't deduct The 6-day Saturdays are prepaid on purpose — taking it off a stipend at payday stings more than paying AED 80 up front. Renewals queue up and wait for an admin's approval; offboarding refunds any unused Saturdays at AED 20 each.
Safeguard · clean books Staff meals are logged to their own expense category, so intern pay never gets muddled with customer-goodwill comps.

The referral path & rewards

A referred friend lands on /r/[code], verifies phone + email right there, and instantly claims a free Welcome Meal — no wizard, no card. When that friend later becomes a paying customer, the person who invited them starts earning across four layers of rewards.

Layer 1
Conversion cash
When the friend first pays, the inviter gets a credit that grows with their track record (~AED 20 → 35). A limited "doubler" can apply.
Layer 2
Cycle milestones
Hitting 3 / 6 / 10 / 15 / 20 recruits in a cycle unlocks mystery cash, free weeks, and cash-plus-skips.
Layer 3
Lifetime tiers
At 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 lifetime recruits: a standing 5% then 10% off, a big meal credit, and a jacket.
Layer 4
Anniversary & extras
An AED 50 credit once a customer passes a year, plus rewards for reviews and surveys.

How credits get spent: they're just AED in a wallet. At checkout they're combined with any loyalty discount into one coupon (loyalty % first, then credit fills the rest). If a credit is bigger than the order, the leftover stays in the wallet for next time.

By design · anniversary is current-year-only The AED 50 anniversary reward is not back-filled for returning dormant customers who missed past years — an owner's call, not a bug.
Safeguard · fraud signals Repeat device/IP patterns and lifetime phone/email checks flag suspicious claims for review; flagged credits stay pending until ops approves them.

What keeps it all safe

Onboarding touches identity and money, so it's wrapped in quiet protections. Here's the plain-English version of what keeps it honest and steady — every one of these is already in place.

The worryHow we handle it
Reused or replayed codesEvery verification code is single-use and short-lived; once spent, it can't sign up anyone else.
"Is this email registered?" probingWe never reveal it — existing users are quietly sent to login instead.
Someone fiddling with the priceThe server re-checks the amount against the official price band; a tampered amount is rejected before any charge.
Double-charges / double-clicksOnly one scheduled plan at a time; paused plans can't check out; credits are reserved so two tabs can't spend the same balance.
The processor re-sending an eventThe payment handler is idempotent — a repeated event won't create a second subscription or re-send messages.
WhatsApp going downThe email fallback keeps sign-up moving; the phone is flagged for verification before delivery.
Referral gamingLifetime phone/email de-dup, per-IP limits, and device/IP signals route shady claims to a review queue.
Delivering somewhere we don't coverUnlisted dorms are blocked at checkout until ops confirms coverage.
A payment that half-finishesA checkpoint lets the handler resume only the unfinished steps on retry, never repeating what's done.
Rule · the prime directive The customer in front of us comes first. Where timeouts or limits exist, they fail gracefully — a hiccup quietly degrades (email fallback, a deferred invoice) rather than blocking the person trying to eat.

E Appendix · Part 2

Verification, pricing & credit engines

Verification

  • WhatsApp codes are HMAC-SHA256 hashed (src/shared/otp-hash.ts), bound to the phone; never stored in plaintext.
  • Single-use via consumed_at; short TTL; resend cooldown + hourly send cap; the verify-attempt counter is atomic against brute force.
  • Email OTP is Supabase Auth (6-digit). The email fallback is honoured only when a real send_failed_at exists; the account is marked whatsapp_verified=false.

Pricing

  • Pure engine contexts/subscriptions/domain/pricing.ts (defaults) + DB overrides via infra/supabase/pricing-repo.ts (plan_pricing).
  • Override priority (Religious): (week,count) → (6DAYS,count) → (week,flat) → (6DAYS,flat); Veg/NonVeg: (week) → (6DAYS).
  • Per-meal is week-type-invariant for Veg/NonVeg; the Religious mix is a weighted average of veg/non-veg days.
  • priceBoundsFils() derives the checkout floor/ceiling from the same engine — the server's tamper check moves in lockstep with admin price changes.

Credits & coupons

  • contexts/dorm-wars/domain/coupon-synth.ts builds one Stripe coupon: loyalty % first, then credit caps the remainder.
  • Credits are reserved (24h) before checkout so two tabs can't double-spend; FIFO consumption with a split-remainder row for leftovers.
  • The webhook is idempotent via orders.webhook_completed_at + per-channel sent markers; a full refund returns credits to the wallet.
